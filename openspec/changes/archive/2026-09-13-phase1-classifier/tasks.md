**Diagnosis of the Filadelfia bosque failure** (`scripts/15_diagnose_filadelfia_bosque.py`, run on the stable n=400 dataset — a per-point breakdown script, not part of the production evaluation pipeline): of 113 true-bosque points in Filadelfia, the CNN misclassifies 61 as pasto/cultivo. Three checks, in order:
1. **Not a period effect** — 2019 (52.6% misclassified) and 2023 (55.4%) fail at nearly identical rates, ruling out a composite/seasonal artifact specific to one year.
2. **RF gets most of the CNN's mistakes right** — of the 61 points the CNN misses, RF correctly calls 41 (67.2%) bosque, predicting the rest as pasto (never cultivo). RF isn't perfect on these points either, but it's clearly more robust than the CNN here.
3. **Band statistics point at fragmented/thin canopy, and the visual check confirms it.** For the RF-important bands (B11/B12 SWIR, B5 red-edge): Filadelfia bosque points the CNN gets *right* look like typical dense canopy (B11=1778±189, close to the AOI training bosque mean of 1934±348, low variance). Points the CNN gets *wrong* have systematically higher SWIR/red-edge reflectance and much higher variance (B11=2103±353) — the spectral signature of thinner, degraded, or edge-affected canopy, not solid forest. **Visual inspection (`data/study_area/filadelfia_bosque_diagnosis.png`) confirms this directly**: CNN-misclassified true-bosque patches are consistently narrow forest strips (windbreaks/live fences between fields — a standard feature of Mennonite colony agriculture) with the 330m window mostly filled by cleared fields and roads; CNN-correct patches are solid, contiguous canopy filling nearly the whole window. **Read**: the CNN learned "bosque" partly as a large contiguous-canopy spatial pattern from the training footprint, which doesn't transfer to Filadelfia's narrow-strip forest remnants — spatial context that's usually informative becomes actively misleading here. RF, reading only the center pixel's spectral values (which stay closer to a forest signature even on thin strips, per the band stats), handles this landscape better precisely because it ignores the misleading spatial context. This is a real, evidenced, landscape-specific mechanism — not a data-volume issue (round 7 above) and not a pipeline bug (round 6).
